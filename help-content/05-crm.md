# CRM — Help Center Collection

**Collection description (for the collection card):**
> Manage leads, deals, tasks, calls, and your entire sales pipeline in one place with the SalesTarget.ai CRM.

Each `## Article N` section below is one Intercom article. UI labels are bolded exactly as they appear in the product.

---

## Article 1 — What is the CRM?

The **CRM** is SalesTarget.ai's built-in workspace for managing leads, deals, and follow-ups. Leads generated from your cold email and LinkedIn campaigns land in the CRM automatically, with their engagement history already attached — so your team can work replies, book meetings, and close deals without switching tools.

### What you can do in the CRM

- **Lead Management** — capture, filter, and enrich every lead in one place
- **Deal Pipeline** — track opportunities through customizable stages
- **Tasks & Follow-Ups** — manage day-to-day sales activities
- **Calling** — call prospects directly from the CRM with the built-in dialer
- **Activity Tracking** — see every email, call, meeting, note, and stage change on the lead's timeline
- **Reports & Analytics** — measure pipeline value, revenue, and activity in real time

### The CRM tabs

Dashboard, Inbox, Lists, Leads, Deals, Products, Orders, Meetings, Tasks, Follow Ups, Notes, Calendar, Tickets, and Reports.

### Who it's for

The CRM is built for outbound sales teams who need their pipeline to move on its own. Because leads arrive from your campaigns with opens, clicks, and replies already logged, reps spend their time on conversations — not admin.

### How it fits into SalesTarget.ai

Find prospects in **Lead Explorer**, reach them with **Campaigns** or **LinkedIn Outreach**, and manage every reply, call, meeting, and deal in the **CRM**.

## Article 2 — How to Use the CRM Dashboard

The **Dashboard** is the first screen you see in the CRM. It gives you a snapshot of your email performance and everything that needs your attention today.

### Email performance cards

The top of the dashboard shows five email stats, updated live:

- **Emails Sent**
- **Emails Delivered**
- **Emails Opened**
- **Emails Replied**
- **Emails Bounced**

### Activity cards

Below the stats are cards that surface what needs attention today:

- **Recommended Leads** — leads worth prioritizing, each with a short reason explaining why it was surfaced
- **Your Tasks** — open tasks with due dates and assignees
- **Meeting for the Day** — today's scheduled meetings
- **Deals** — recent and upcoming deals
- **Notes** — latest notes

**Your Tasks**, **Meeting for the Day**, **Deals**, and **Notes** each include a quick-create button, so you can add a task, meeting, deal, or note without switching tabs. **Recommended Leads** has a **View Leads** button that takes you to the Leads tab.

### Rearranging your dashboard

You can **drag the activity cards** into whatever order suits your workflow. Your layout is remembered, so the next time you open the dashboard it looks the way you left it.

### Tips

- Use the dashboard as your morning check-in — email trends and today's meetings sit side by side.
- A rising **Emails Bounced** number is an early warning to validate your lists.
- Recommended Leads is scored on badges, opens, and replies — it's the fastest way to find who's worth calling today.

## Article 3 — How to Manage Leads in the CRM

The **Leads** tab is your master database of every lead across campaigns, lists, and imports.

### Understanding the leads table

Each row shows the lead's **Name**, **Email**, source **Provider** (email or LinkedIn), **Company**, email **Status**, a qualification **Badge**, the source **Campaign**, engagement icons, and **Phone**.

A few details worth knowing:

- For **LinkedIn leads**, the Company cell also shows the job title and a link to their profile.
- The **Phone** column shows a green **Call** button rather than the number — click it to call the lead directly through the built-in dialer. See **How to Make Calls from the CRM**.

Click a lead's name to open their full profile.

### Email statuses

Queued, Delivered, Opened, Clicked, Bounced, and Replied.

### Filtering your leads

1. Search by name or email.
2. Narrow the table with the **Campaign**, **List**, and **Status** filters.
3. Use **Advanced Filters** to combine an email status with a frequency — for example **Opened** plus **More than 2 times** to find your most engaged leads.

Advanced Filters offers status chips (Queued, Delivered, Opened, Clicked, Bounced, Replied) and frequency chips (**More than 2 times**, **Exactly 2 times**, **Once**).

### Selecting leads

Click the header checkbox to choose how much to select:

- **Select this page**
- **Select all {N} leads**
- **Select first N** — a custom number

### Bulk actions

With leads selected, you can:

- **Create Meeting**, **Create Task**, or **Create Deal** for the selection
- **Export** the selection to CSV
- **Move To List**

You can also create a brand-new list from your selection.

### About badges

Badges are your own qualification labels, separate from the automatic email status. **Badges are set from the campaign's leads screen** — open the campaign, find the lead, and set the badge there. The CRM Leads table and lead profile display badges but don't currently offer an editor.

### Tips

- Filter for leads who opened more than twice — that's your warmest follow-up segment.
- Use pagination controls to show 20, 50, or 100 leads per page.

## Article 4 — How to Use the Lead Profile

Click any lead's name in the **Leads** tab to open their profile — the complete record of who they are and every interaction you've had with them.

### Left panel: lead details

The profile shows the lead's **Name, Job Title, Company, Phone, LinkedIn, Location, Status,** and source **Provider**. You can edit fields directly.

Expand **Company Information** to see Domain, Industry, City, Country, Revenue, **Employees**, LinkedIn, Campaign, and List where available.

### Quick actions

Work a lead without leaving the profile:

- **Email** — compose and send from a connected email account
- **Call** — call the lead through the built-in dialer
- **LinkedIn** — open the lead's LinkedIn profile
- **Note**, **Meeting**, **Task**, **Deal** — create a linked record in one click

Under the **More** menu you'll also find:

- **Log Email** and **Log Meeting** — record activity that happened outside SalesTarget.ai so the timeline stays complete
- **Create Ticket** — raise a support ticket linked to this lead

### Right panel: history tabs

- **Timeline** — a chronological feed of every activity on the lead, shared with your whole team
- **Call Notes** — notes and outcomes from calls made through the dialer
- **Tasks**, **Deals**, **Orders**, **Meetings**, **Notes**, **Tickets**, **Follow Ups** — everything linked to the lead

### Tips

- Use **Log Email** and **Log Meeting** to keep the timeline complete for conversations that happened off-platform.
- Because the timeline is shared, anyone picking up a lead can see the full history without asking.
- Overdue follow-ups are flagged directly on the profile.

## Article 5 — How to Create & Manage Lists in the CRM

Lists let you organize leads into named groups — by campaign, segment, territory, or any structure that fits your workflow.

### Create a list

1. Open **CRM → Lists**.
2. Click **Add New List** and choose either:
   - **Add New List** — name an empty list, or
   - **Upload Lists File** — upload a CSV of leads into a new list.
3. Your new list appears with its **Name**, **Date**, **Created By**, and **Total Leads** count.

### Work with a list

- Search lists by name, filter by creator using **All Members**, and sort by newest or oldest.
- Download a list as a CSV from the row action.
- Select lists and click **Delete** to remove them.

### Inside a list

Click a list to open it and see every lead with their **Name, Email, Company,** and **Job Title**. From here you can:

- Click **Add Leads** to add more
- Select leads to create a deal, meeting, or task for the selection
- Remove leads from the list

### Tips

- Name lists consistently — including segment and month makes them easy to find later.
- Removing a lead from a list doesn't delete the lead; it remains in your **Leads** tab.

## Article 6 — How to Import & Export Leads in the CRM

You can move leads into and out of the CRM at any time — from CSV files, from your campaigns, or out to other tools.

### Import leads from a CSV

1. Open **CRM → Lists** and choose **Add New List → Upload Lists File**.
2. Name the list and upload your CSV.
3. Your file must include an **Email** column. A downloadable template is available if you want the recommended format.
4. Confirm the column mapping. The leads land in your new list and appear in the **Leads** tab.

You can also import from the **Leads** tab using the **Import Lists File** option in the List filter.

### Import from HubSpot or Salesforce

Importing from another CRM is a two-part process:

1. **Connect the account** — go to **Settings → Integrations** and connect HubSpot or Salesforce.
2. **Run the import** — open a CRM list, click **Add Leads**, and choose **Import from Salesforce** or **Import from HubSpot**.

### Leads from your campaigns

Leads from your email and LinkedIn campaigns flow into the CRM **automatically**, with engagement history attached. No manual import needed.

### Export leads to CSV

1. In the **Leads** tab, select the leads you want.
2. Click **Export** to download a CSV of the selection.

You can also download an entire list from **CRM → Lists**.

### Tips

- Keep a consistent CSV header format (Name, Email, Company, Job Title, Phone) so imports map cleanly.
- Campaign leads don't need CSV import — they arrive automatically.

## Article 7 — How to Manage Deals & Your Sales Pipeline

The **Deals** tab is your sales pipeline — every opportunity, its stage, its value, and its expected close date.

### Create a deal

1. Open **CRM → Deals** and click **Add Deal**.
2. Fill in the required fields: deal name, the linked lead's email, the **Stage**, the **Member** it's assigned to, and the **Amount**.
3. Optionally add an **Estimated Close Date**, plus Pipeline, Deal Type, Priority, Description, Next Step, or a Closed Won/Lost Reason.
4. Optionally associate Products, Orders, Tickets, Tasks, and Meetings.

### Default pipeline stages

Out of the box the pipeline uses eight stages:

**Lead → Sales Qualified → Meeting Booked → Negotiation → Contract Sent → Closed Won → Closed Lost → Not Interested**

### Work the pipeline

The pipeline is organized as columns, one per stage. Drag a deal between columns to update its stage. You can also add a deal directly into a column, and open any card to edit or delete it.

### Customize your stages

Use the stage manager to add a stage (with a name and colour), rename or recolour existing stages, drag to reorder, or delete stages you don't use.

### Table view

Switch to table view for a sortable list with columns for **Deal Name, Email, Created By, Assigned To, Stage, Amount, Close Date,** and **Create Date**. Use **Edit Columns** to add custom fields.

Stage and owner filters are available in table view. In kanban view, the columns themselves act as the stage filter.

### Tips

- Keep **Amount** and **Close Date** current — Pipeline Value and Revenue in Reports pull directly from your deal data.
- Create deals in bulk from the **Leads** tab by selecting leads and clicking **Create Deal**.

## Article 8 — How to Manage Tasks in the CRM

The **Tasks** tab keeps your to-dos organized and linked to the records they belong to.

### Create a task

1. Open **CRM → Tasks** and click **Add Task**.
2. Fill in the required fields: task name, description, **Due Date**, **Assignees**, **Status**, and **Priority**.
3. Optionally add a List, Task Type, Reminder, Queue, Outcome, or Next Step.
4. Optionally associate the task with a Company, Contacts, Deals, Meetings, Tasks, Tickets, Notes, Products, or Orders.

**Assignees** is a multi-select — you can assign a task to more than one person.

You can also create tasks from a lead's profile, from selected leads in the **Leads** tab, or from the dashboard.

### Track your tasks

- Use the board view to see tasks by status, and drag cards between columns to update status.
- Use the table view for a sortable list with columns for title, owner, status, priority, due date, and assignee.
- Filter by status, priority, or owner, and search by title.

### Customize statuses

Default statuses are **To Do, In Progress, Deferred,** and **Completed**. You can add, rename, recolour, reorder, or delete statuses to match how your team works.

### Tips

- Set a priority on every task — it makes the board scannable at a glance.
- Tasks linked to a lead also appear on that lead's profile, so the whole team sees them in context.
- Overdue tasks are surfaced in the **Activity** section of Reports.

## Article 9 — How to Schedule & Manage Meetings

The **Meetings** area tracks every meeting your team books — where it happens, who's hosting, and how it fits into the pipeline.

### Create a meeting

1. Go to **CRM → Meetings** and click **Add Meeting**.
2. Fill in the required fields: meeting name, the linked lead's email, **Meeting Venue**, date, start and end times, and host(s).
3. Optionally add an agenda, internal notes, or link to deals and contacts.

**Meeting Venue** determines which column the meeting appears in on the board. The default venues are **Online**, **In Office**, and **Client Location**, and you can customize them using the **Venues** manager.

You can also book a meeting from a lead's profile or by selecting leads in the **Leads** tab.

### Connect your calendar

Once **Google Calendar** is connected, meetings you book in the CRM sync to your real calendar, and Google Meet links are generated automatically.

Supported meeting platforms are **Zoom**, **Google Meet**, and **Calendly**.

### Organize and review

Meetings appear on the meetings board and on the **CRM Calendar**. Today's meetings also surface on the dashboard's **Meeting for the Day** card.

### Tips

- Connect **Google Calendar** first — it makes every meeting you book show up where your team already looks.
- Use **Log Meeting** from the lead profile's **More** menu to record meetings that happened off-platform.
- Create a follow-up task after each meeting so next steps don't get lost.

## Article 10 — How to Use Follow-ups

Follow-ups are lightweight reminders to re-engage a lead — a simpler, date-driven companion to tasks.

### Create a follow-up

1. Go to **CRM → Follow Ups** and click **Add Follow-up**.
2. Enter the required fields: follow-up name, **Follow-up Date**, **Status**, and **Priority**.
3. Optionally add a description, follow-up time, the linked lead, an assigned member, a **Channel**, a follow-up type, a reminder, an outcome, and a **Next Step**.

You can also add a follow-up directly from a lead's profile.

### Track your follow-ups

- Use the board view to see follow-ups by status; drag between columns to update.
- Switch to the table view for a sortable list.
- Default statuses are **Pending**, **Completed**, and **Cancelled**.

A follow-up that stays open past its date is flagged as **overdue** on the lead's profile, and overdue counts appear in the **Activity** section of Reports.

### Tips

- Create a follow-up the moment a lead says "not now" — set the date for when they said to check back.
- Use the **Channel** field to plan the touch (email, call, LinkedIn) so future-you doesn't have to decide.

## Article 11 — How to Take Notes in the CRM

Notes capture the context that doesn't fit in a field — call summaries, objections, buying signals, internal commentary — and are visible to your whole team.

### Create a note

1. Open **CRM → Notes** and click **Add Note**.
2. Write your note in the editor and set a **Status** (both are required).
3. Optionally set a Note Type, Category, Priority, or Reminder.
4. Optionally link the note to a Company, Contacts, Deals, Meetings, Tasks, Tickets, or Orders.

You can also add notes from a lead's profile — through the **Note** quick action or the profile's **Notes** tab — and from the dashboard.

### Organize your notes

- Use the board view to group notes by status; drag between columns to reclassify.
- Switch to the table view for a sortable list.
- Default statuses are **Note**, **Follow Up**, and **Important**. Customize them to match your workflow — for example *Call Summary*, *Objection*, or *Pricing*.

### Tips

- Write a note immediately after every call while details are fresh.
- Notes linked to a lead appear on that lead's profile, so anyone picking up the conversation has the full story.

## Article 12 — How to Manage Tickets in the CRM

The **Tickets** area tracks customer issues and requests through to resolution.

### Create a ticket

1. Go to **CRM → Tickets** and click **Add Ticket**.
2. Fill in the required fields: ticket name, **Ticket Status**, description, **Source**, **Ticket Owner**, **Priority**, and **Create Date**.
3. Optionally add tags, a due date, category, resolution notes, an SLA, sentiment, or a ticket type.
4. Optionally link the ticket to a Company, Contacts, Deals, Tasks, Meetings, Notes, or Orders.

You can also raise a ticket from a lead's profile via the **More** menu → **Create Ticket**.

### Work the ticket board

Default statuses are **Open**, **In Progress**, **Resolved**, and **Closed**. Use the board view to move tickets through your workflow, or the table view for a sortable list you can filter by priority or owner.

### Tips

- Always set the source — it tells you which channels generate the most volume.
- Use tags for recurring themes (billing, bug, onboarding).
- Fill in the resolution before closing a ticket; it becomes searchable history.

## Article 13 — How to Manage Products in the CRM

The **Products** area is your catalog — the things you sell, priced and ready to attach to deals and orders.

### Create a product

1. Go to **CRM → Products** and click **Add Product**.
2. Enter the product name, SKU, description, price, and currency (USD, EUR, GBP, INR, AUD, or CAD).
3. Optionally add unit cost and billing frequency.

When you enter both a price and a unit cost, the **margin is calculated automatically**.

### Organize the catalog

Group products by billing frequency — **One-time, Monthly, Quarterly, Yearly, Custom**, or **No Billing**. Filter by active or inactive status.

Deleting a product **deactivates** it rather than removing it, so historical records stay intact.

### Use products in deals and orders

When creating a deal or an order, add products from your catalog as line items with quantity, price, and calculated total.

### Tips

- Set unit cost as well as price so margins are visible.
- Keep retired products inactive rather than deleted.
- Use consistent SKU conventions.

## Article 14 — How to Manage Orders in the CRM

The **Orders** area records what customers have actually bought.

### Create an order

1. Go to **CRM → Orders** and click **Create Order**.
2. Enter the order name and stage.
3. Attach the lead and add line items from your product catalog.
4. Confirm the **Total Price**, **Assign To**, and **Order Date**. Optionally add notes, a discount, tax, or billing details.

### Track order status

Orders move through a fixed workflow:

**Draft → Confirmed → Invoiced → Paid** (or **Cancelled**)

Update status on the board or in the table view. The table also shows an **Order #** column and association columns for Deals, Notes, and Products.

### Tips

- Create the order as soon as a deal reaches **Closed Won**.
- Use the **Discount** field on the order rather than editing product prices.

## Article 15 — How to Use the CRM Calendar

The **Calendar** tab brings your meetings, tasks, follow-ups, and deal close dates into one time view.

### Reading the calendar

Events are colour-coded by type — meetings, tasks, follow-ups, and deals (plotted by estimated close date) — with a legend. Switch between **Day**, **Week**, and **Month** views, and click any event to open its details.

In Month view, days with more than three events collapse to a **+N more** indicator.

### Filter what you see

Use the calendar settings to control which entity types and statuses are visible — for example, hide completed tasks or show only pending follow-ups.

### Tips

- Start your morning in **Day** view — it's effectively your sales agenda.
- Use **Month** view with only deals visible for a close-date forecast at a glance.

## Article 16 — How to Use the CRM Inbox

The **Inbox** tab gives you a unified view of every lead reply across your connected email accounts, so you can read and answer messages without switching tools.

### Work replies from inside the CRM

1. Open **CRM → Inbox**.
2. Use the folder tabs — **All**, **Unread**, **Sent**, **Spam**, **Trash** — and filter by inbox account, campaign, or status.
3. Open a conversation to read the full thread.
4. **Reply**, **Reply All**, or **Forward** directly from the connected account that received the message. You can also compose a new email and attach files (under 10 MB each).

If a sender isn't in your CRM yet, use the **Add Lead To CRM** button in the message header to capture them straight from the conversation.

The CRM Inbox is the same tool as **Unibox** — see **How to Use Unibox** for the full guide.

### From reply to pipeline

When a lead replies with interest:

1. Open the lead in the **Leads** tab (search their email).
2. Use the profile's quick actions to create a **Deal**, schedule a **Meeting**, set a **Follow-up**, or **Call** them.

Because campaign engagement flows into the CRM automatically, the reply is already reflected on the lead's timeline.

To update a lead's **Badge**, open the campaign the lead came from and set it on the campaign's leads screen.

### Tips

- Work the Inbox top-to-bottom at set times of day, converting each positive reply into a deal or meeting before moving on.
- You can also send one-off emails from a lead's profile with the **Email** quick action.

## Article 17 — How to Make Calls from the CRM

The CRM includes a built-in dialer, so you can call prospects without leaving the platform — and every call is logged against the lead automatically.

### Ways to start a call

**From the Leads table** — click the green **Call** button in the lead's **Phone** column.

**From a lead's profile** — click the **Call** quick action.

**From the Dialer page** — click **Dialer** in the left sidebar for the full calling workspace.

### The call widget

When a call starts, a call widget appears and stays visible while you work, so you can look at the lead's profile, timeline, and previous notes during the conversation.

### Call notes

Notes you take during or after a call are saved to the lead and appear in the **Call Notes** tab on their profile. This gives you and your team the full calling history for every lead in one place.

### Tips

- **Check the lead's timeline before dialling** — knowing they opened your last three emails changes how you open the call.
- **Write your note immediately after hanging up**, while the details are fresh.
- **Create a follow-up or task straight from the profile** while you're still on the lead's record.
- Use the dashboard's **Recommended Leads** card to build your call list — it surfaces leads based on engagement.

## Article 18 — How to Read CRM Reports

The **Reports** tab gives you a live read on how your pipeline is performing. It's built from your live data, so it updates in real time without anyone having to prepare a report.

### Choose a time range

Filter the dashboard by **Today**, **This Week**, **This Month**, or **This Year**. Every card and chart updates to the selected window.

### KPI cards

- **New Leads**
- **Open Deals**
- **Pipeline Value**
- **Revenue**
- **Win Rate**

### Leads

- **Lead Funnel** — your leads grouped by badge
- **New Leads Trend** — how lead volume is moving over time

### Deals

- **Pipeline by Stage** — where your revenue is sitting and how it's distributed
- **Deals Created** — new deal volume over the period

### Orders

- **Revenue Over Time** — realized revenue from orders
- **Orders by Status**
- **Top Products by Revenue** — which products actually drive your revenue

### Activity

- **Meetings**
- **Tasks, Tickets & Follow-ups** — including **overdue** counts and tickets grouped by priority

### Email Performance

**Open Rate**, **Reply Rate**, **Bounce Rate**, and **Click Rate**.

### Tips

- Pipeline Value depends on keeping deal **Amounts**, **Stages**, and **Close Dates** current — reports are only as good as your data.
- **Top Products by Revenue** is the fastest way to see what's genuinely selling versus what you talk about most.
- Watch overdue tasks and follow-ups weekly. A rising overdue count is the earliest sign your team is overloaded.
- A healthy funnel narrows gradually; a sharp drop between two stages tells you where your process leaks.

## Article 19 — How to Customize the CRM (Stages, Forms & Columns)

The CRM adapts to your process. Three customization tools apply across the modules where they make sense.

### 1. Customize stages and statuses

Every board's columns are editable. You can:

- Add a new stage with a name and colour
- Rename or recolour existing stages
- Reorder columns by dragging
- Delete stages you don't use

Deal pipeline stages are the most common thing to customize. Note that for **Meetings**, the equivalent manager is called **Venues**.

### 2. Customize create forms

Each entity's create form has an editor that lets you:

- Turn optional properties on or off (for example, add **Priority** or **Next Step** to deals)
- Choose which related records (Company, Contacts, Products) can be linked at creation
- **Save** to apply the layout for your team, or **Reset** to restore defaults

Form editing is permission-gated — if you don't have access, the editor will tell you.

### 3. Customize table columns

In any table view, click **Edit Columns** to open **Choose which columns you see**:

- Keep the core columns visible
- Add optional custom fields and associations
- Drag columns to reorder, then click **Apply**

Your column layout is saved per entity, as is your board-or-table view choice for each tab.

### Tips

- Mirror your real sales process in your deal stages before importing leads — clean stage history is easier than renaming after the fact.
- Keep create forms minimal; every extra field slows down data entry.

## Article 20 — CRM Integrations

Connect the CRM to the rest of your stack.

### Where to find them

Go to **Settings → Integrations → All Integrations**. Filter by category using the **All**, **Calendar**, **Communication**, and **Automation** chips.

### Available integrations

**Calendar & scheduling**

- **Google Calendar** — sync CRM meetings to your calendar
- **Google Meet** — meeting links generated through your Google Calendar connection
- **Calendly** — connect your scheduling links to create Calendly meetings from the CRM
- **Zoom** — create Zoom meetings from the CRM

**Communication**

- **Slack** — receive CRM notifications in your workspace channels, including meeting reminders sent to a channel you choose

**Automation & data**

- **Zapier** — connect the CRM to thousands of apps
- **Salesforce** — import your Salesforce leads into the CRM
- **HubSpot** — import your HubSpot contacts
- **Pipedrive** — *coming soon*

### Connect an integration

1. Open **Settings → Integrations** and browse by category.
2. Click **Connect** on the integration you want. (For **Zapier**, click **Setup** to open the setup dialog.)
3. Approve the authorization prompt for OAuth apps like Google, Zoom, and Slack.
4. Connected integrations show a **Connected** badge. You can disconnect at any time.

**Google Meet** shares the same connection as Google Calendar — connecting Google Calendar enables both.

### Importing from Salesforce or HubSpot

Connecting the account in Settings is only the first step. To actually import contacts, open a CRM list, click **Add Leads**, and choose **Import from Salesforce** or **Import from HubSpot**.

### Tips

- Connect **Google Calendar** first — every meeting you book in the CRM then shows up where your team already looks.
- Use **Salesforce** or **HubSpot** import when migrating, then make SalesTarget.ai your single source of truth.
- With **Zapier**, push new CRM leads to any tool that isn't natively integrated.
